England rounded off their 2025 autumn international programme with an unbeaten clean sweep through the Quilter Nations Series, defeating Australia, Fiji, New Zealand and Argentina across four Twickenham fixtures in November. The results represented England's most positive autumn window in several seasons and prompted genuine optimism ahead of the 2026 Six Nations Championship.

## How the Autumn Played Out

England opened the series on 1 November with a well-organised 25-7 defeat of Australia. The Wallabies were limited to a single try as England's defensive structure and kicking game proved the difference. It was a composed rather than spectacular performance, but it set the tone for what followed.

The wins over Fiji and New Zealand reinforced England's growing sense of identity under Steve Borthwick's coaching set-up. The victory over the All Blacks was particularly significant — New Zealand had beaten England in recent meetings and arriving at Twickenham with any kind of winning record over the Southern Hemisphere giants is cause for confidence.

The final match of the series, against Argentina, produced a harder-fought contest. The Pumas pushed England in the closing stages before the hosts held firm to complete their unbeaten autumn.

## Why This Matters for the Six Nations

The 2026 Six Nations is a different competition entirely. The scheduling is relentless — five matches in seven weeks — and the atmospheres in Dublin, Edinburgh, Paris and Cardiff are categorically more hostile than a home crowd on a Saturday in November.

That said, England enter the tournament having beaten four Test nations in a row. Their defensive organisation, set-piece accuracy and squad depth have all improved. Maro Itoje leads the side with clear authority, and there is genuine competition for places across the squad — a sign of a healthy programme.

The key tests will come when England face Ireland at Twickenham in Round 3 and then travel to France for the final fixture. Those two games are likely to decide whether England are genuine Six Nations contenders or simply a side that performed well in autumn conditions.

## A Squad Built for the Long Term

What stands out from this England group is the youth alongside experience. Henry Pollock has emerged as a genuine openside option. Immanuel Feyi-Waboso has given England a cutting edge out wide. And the set-piece, anchored by Jamie George and Ellis Genge, remains one of the best in the northern hemisphere.

Steve Borthwick has spoken consistently about England being a work in progress — building toward the 2027 Rugby World Cup in Australia. The autumn results suggest that progress is accelerating.

## What It Means for Clubs and Players Across the UK

England's success on the international stage energises the domestic game. When England win — particularly over iconic opposition like New Zealand — club registrations tend to rise and junior sections see increased interest.
