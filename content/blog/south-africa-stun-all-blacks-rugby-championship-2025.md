South Africa produced one of their most commanding performances against New Zealand in years, running in six tries to record a stunning 43-10 victory in Wellington during the fourth round of the 2025 Rugby Championship. The result was a serious statement of intent from Rassie Erasmus's side, who had been under pressure following back-to-back defeats earlier in the tournament.

## A Slow Start Then a Second-Half Demolition

The first half was a tightly contested affair. New Zealand went into the break with a slender advantage after Leroy Carter crossed the line and Damian McKenzie added a penalty. The All Blacks, playing at their Sky Stadium fortress in Wellington, looked to have enough of a platform to build on.

What followed in the second half was remarkable. Cheslin Kolbe — one of the most dangerous finishers in world rugby — scored twice, his first coming just before half-time and his second immediately after the restart. Manie Libbok, who ran the show brilliantly from fly-half, converted both and added a penalty goal to extend the lead.

The Springboks then cut loose. Damian Willemse, Kwagga Smith and RG Snyman all crossed in a 20-minute period that effectively ended the match as a contest. Andre Esterhuizen added a sixth try in the final minute, with Libbok landing five conversions to finish the game's outstanding individual performer.

## What the Result Means

The 33-point winning margin left New Zealand sitting on 10 points at the end of the match — their heaviest home defeat in the tournament for many years. South Africa, by contrast, suddenly looked like the side capable of retaining the title they had won the previous year.

At the time of the match, both teams sat level on points in the Rugby Championship table, with the title race running deep into October. The final round saw South Africa pip New Zealand on points difference, retaining the championship for the second consecutive year.

## Kolbe and Libbok the Difference-Makers

Cheslin Kolbe has long been recognised as one of the most dangerous backs in the world, and his performance in Wellington underlined just how important he is to the Springbok system. His pace and instinct to find space in what appeared tight defensive situations was exceptional.

Manie Libbok at fly-half controlled the pace and tempo of the game effectively, mixing kicks with quick ball and demonstrating a composure that was in sharp contrast to the errors that have occasionally plagued him in previous high-pressure Tests.

## New Zealand's Afternoon to Forget

For the All Blacks, the defeat was a chastening experience. Scott Robertson's side had been competitive but were simply outclassed once South Africa found their rhythm. Discipline cost them territory in the third quarter, and the failure to deal with Kolbe's threat on the edge proved catastrophic.

The result left New Zealand needing to win their remaining matches convincingly to close the points difference gap — a task that ultimately proved just beyond them when South Africa secured the title.

## What It Means for Grassroots Rugby Fans

Test matches at this level have a real trickle-down effect on the grassroots game. When the Rugby Championship is competitive, enthusiasm builds across UK clubs, particularly through the autumn international window that follows. Many players and coaches at clubs around the UK follow the southern hemisphere game closely for tactical insights and inspiration.
